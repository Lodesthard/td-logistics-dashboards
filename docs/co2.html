Which routes should we prioritize to cut network CO2?

For: sustainability/CSR manager. Decision this supports: which specific delivery routes are the best candidates for consolidation or a shift to rail, and which warehouse is the least CO2-efficient per tonne delivered.

Highest-CO2 routes (ranking → bar chart)

A ranked list of specific, actionable routes is exactly what a bar chart is for — each bar is one route someone could actually change.

Where emissions concentrate (spatial → scatter over a real France outline)

This decision is inherently about geography — which region to target for consolidation — so a plain ranking isn't enough; we plot each city at its real coordinates, colored by its CO2 contribution, over a real (simplified) outline of France so "where" reads at a glance instead of requiring you to mentally place bare longitude/latitude numbers. Existing warehouses get their own marker shape (a triangle), not just a color — a shape difference survives grayscale printing and color-blindness in a way color alone doesn't, and it answers a second real question at a glance: is CO2 concentrating near a warehouse (fixable by re-routing) or far from every warehouse (fixable only by opening a new one)?

How many routes do we need to act on? (Pareto chart: sorted bars + cumulative line)

Step 15 — added chart. The top-10 bar ranks routes but doesn't say where to stop: is the CO2 concentrated in 3 routes or spread over 15? That's the real planning question for a CSR manager with a limited budget for consolidation/rail-shift projects. A Pareto chart answers it directly: every route sorted by CO2 (bars), plus the running share of network CO2 (line, right axis), so "act on the first N routes to cover X% of emissions" is read off one curve. A pie can't show the cumulative reading, and the existing top-10 bar stops at 10 and hides the long tail.
